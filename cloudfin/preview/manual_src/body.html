Introduction

AERIA OS is an operating system for the Octatrack MKII, written by sadnoise. It takes the place of the instrument's own operating system and turns the same panel, the same sockets and the same sound processor into a different instrument: a live multi-effect built around a sky, and an eight-track sequencer.

The two faces of AERIA OS

{{DIAGRAM faces}}

The sky is the multi-effect. What you play into inputs A to D runs through a chain of up to seven effects. The weather moves their knobs. Three sources feed the chain as well: a loop, a ring of one-shot samples called stars, and balloons, which are recordings you catch from the inputs. Four MIDI modules play other instruments. The crossfader moves the sound between two scenes.

The sequencer is a groove box: eight tracks on the sixteen trigs. Each track has a voice (a sample, or one of four small synthesizers, or MIDI alone), a high-pass and a low-pass filter, and room for one effect from the same list the sky uses.

[FUNC] + [MIDI] goes from one face to the other. Both share the master section (a freeze delay, a four-band EQ, bass mono and a limiter on the main output), the tempo, the projects that keep your work under a name, the card, USB and the screensavers.

Overview of AERIA OS's structure

This chapter shows how the parts fit together: where the sound goes in each face, what the two faces share, and what is kept when the power goes off. The later chapters take each part in turn.

The sky: the signal's way

  1. What goes in. Inputs A B and C D, each pair with its own gain and each stereo or mono, and the three sources: LOOP, STARS and BALLOONS, each with a SEND level ({{ch:sources}}). DRIV on the GAIN page drives the sum into the chain.
  2. The chain. Seven slots in a row. Each holds one of the sixteen effects or is empty ({{ch:chain}}, {{ch:fx}}). Five of the sixteen belong to the weather, which puts them into empty slots by itself ({{pg:wxfx}}). The unit's sound processor, the DSP, has two cores; AERIA OS places each effect on a core that has room, and refuses an effect that fits on neither.
  3. Branches. With OUT, a slot can be taken out of the line: it still hears the chain at its place, but plays to the MAIN pair, the CUE pair, or anywhere among the four outputs with its own quad panner ({{ch:out}}).
  4. The main feedback takes the chain's output back to its input: FDBK, FTON and WARM on the MIX page.
  5. The weather filters. Rain, thunder, lightning and helium act on the chain's output directly, apart from moving the effects' knobs ({{ch:sky}}).
  6. The master section, in this order: the freeze delay, the output level and balance, the four-band EQ, bass mono, the limiter. The weather never moves any of it ({{ch:mix}}).
  7. The outputs. MAIN carries the master's result, and so do USB channels 5 and 6. CUE carries what was routed there. The headphones carry both.

The sequencer: the signal's way

  1. A machine makes the track's sound each time a step fires: a sample, or one of four small synthesizers, or no sound at all for a track that only sends MIDI ({{ch:seqpages}}). The step brings its velocity, its locks, its ratchet, its chance and its condition ({{ch:seqsteps}}).
  2. Level. Still on the main processor: the track's volume and the ducking. The DLY send to the master's freeze delay is taken here, after the level and before the filters.
  3. A channel of its own in the DSP. Each track goes to the sound processor as its own channel: a high-pass and a low-pass filter that are always there, then one effect, then the pan and the choice of output pair.
  4. The stem of a track is tapped after its effect and before its pan. With the 18-channel USB format selected on the computer, the eight stems are recorded beside the main mix ({{ch:usb}}).
  5. The master section is the same one the sky uses. Here the freeze delay hears only what the tracks send it.
  6. Inputs A to D pass to the output dry while the sequencer is showing. The sky's chain, its sources and its weather filters are not heard.
A track reaches its effect in mono and is panned after it. An effect whose two sides differ (PITCH with WIDE, GRAINS with SPRD, RING, RESO, SMEAR, ROSE, VERB, ECHO, VOWL with WIDE) is therefore the only stereo width a track has. A track with no effect is mono, and so is its stem.

What is kept, and where

  • The saved state is for carrying on where you stopped. AERIA OS writes it by itself: the sound about two seconds after you stop changing things, the sequence at every touch.   • The samples are not copied anywhere. The unit remembers which files were loaded and reads them from the card again at start-up and when a project loads.

Quick start

Two short walks, one for each face. Each step shows the keys to press and what the screen should look like afterwards. Connect something to inputs A B (a synthesizer, a microphone preamp, a drum machine) and listen on the main outputs or the headphones.

Switching on

Switch on. AERIA's intro paints the logo with a brush and writes BY SADNOISE. The screen flashes once, the sea rises, and the sky opens. The intro is silent. Press any key to skip it. You can turn the intro off on the SYSTEM page ({{ch:system}}).

[[G3]] intro1 | The brush paints the logo intro2 | BY SADNOISE is typed under it intro3 | The logo fades and the sea rises [[/G]]

AERIA OS comes back as you left it ({{pg:kept}}). The chain starts with PITCH in slot 1, GRAINS, RING and SMEAR in slots 3 to 5, and slots 2, 6 and 7 empty, which leaves room for what the weather lends. The very first time, all four start dry: each MIX is held at 0 and PITCH is set to no shift, so you hear your sound as it comes in.

If something goes wrong at start-up, see {{ch:update}}. The Startup Menu (hold [FUNC] while switching on) is always there. Never press TRIG 2 (EMPTY RESET) there unless you mean to wipe the unit.

A first flight in the sky

[[STEP 1 | SRC TRIG1-7=slots | qs_sky_chain]]

Open the chain. Press [SRC]. The seven slots hang on a string; slot 1, PITCH, is selected. Play: you hear your sound as it is, because every effect is dry.

[[/STEP]] [[STEP 2 | TRIG3 F=MIX | qs_sky_mix]]

Bring an effect in. Press [TRIG 3] to select GRAINS. Turn ((F)), MIX, up to about half. The popup shows the value. Your sound is now cut into grains.

[[/STEP]] [[STEP 3 | A=SIZE B=DENS C=POS D=SPRY E=PTCH | chain_popup_ms]]

[[/STEP]] [[STEP 4 | AMP TRIG3=RAIN TRIG1=CLEAR | qs_sky_rain]]

Add the master's delay. Press [MIX] for the MASTER page. Turn ((F)), WET, up. The sixteen trigs set the delay's time in sixteenth notes.

[[/STEP]] [[STEP 6 | XFADER=freeze | master_freeze]]

The crossfader

The crossfader does two things.

  • Scenes. It moves every knob held in scene A toward its place in scene B, and back. With no knob in either scene it changes nothing (chapter 13).
  • Freeze. Pushed most of the way to B it freezes the master's delay; brought back, the delay lets go. XFRZ on the MASTER page turns this on or off; it is on at first (chapter 12).

It no longer blends two weathers. Moving the crossfader does not wake a screensaver.

The screen is a landscape: clouds traced from sadnoise's drawings drift in the wind over the water, with a boat, a sun or a moon, birds, stars and balloons. The weather is the heart of AERIA. One weather by itself

These choose a single weather and turn the others off:

  • Turn LEVEL, or press ▲ ▼, on any page: the next or the previous one, in the order CLEAR, CLOUDY, RAIN, STORM, SNOW, HEAT, FOG.

What the weather does to the effects

Each weather has a character, and it sets the knobs of every effect in the chain to suit. It goes by the knob's name: SIZE, TIME, DECY, FDBK, TONE, RATE, RES, WDTH, DPTH, SMR, FRZ, GRN, DENS, WIDE, TILT, BRIT, GATE, DTUN, MOD and FOLW follow the weather. A knob with another name (a pitch, a root, a frequency) sits at the effect's own setting until you turn it, and a storm throws it too. MIX never follows the weather: how much of an effect you hear is yours alone.

With several weathers on, each knob goes to the average of the places those weathers would give it. With none on, it goes to CLEAR's place.

A knob follows the weather until you turn it. Then it is yours. Push the knob on CHAIN to give it back. When a weather is turned on or off, every knob of the kinds listed above goes back to following the weather, and glides to its new place one step at a time. A knob held by a scene stands where the scene puts it instead (chapter 13).

The weather filters keep a low resonance on purpose, so they colour the sound rather than whistle.

The weather as sound

The weather also acts on the chain's output directly:

  • RAIN and STORM: a low-pass filter (it removes highs) eases in to about 6 kHz over a few seconds, and out again when the rain stops.
  • CLOUDY and STORM: thunder. Now and then the low-pass is pulled down and rolls back up. A lightning strike pulls it down to about 700 Hz.
  • Lightning (trig 16 on SKY, MIX, GAIN and CHAIN; it also strikes by itself in a STORM): the screen and the lamps flash, the bright knobs (TONE, WDTH) duck and recover, and the thunder rolls.
  • The storm throws the knobs. In a STORM every knob of every effect that you are not holding is thrown somewhere new, each by itself, every two to nine seconds, and all of them at once when lightning strikes. Knobs with a weather rule stay inside its range (feedback and resonance keep their ceilings); the others take their whole range. MIX and the knobs you hold are left alone. When the storm ends they settle back to the other weathers' places.
  • Lightning lends effects. Each strike of a STORM drops a random effect the chain does not have into an empty slot for 45 to 90 seconds; the popup says LIGHTNING LENDS and its name. Strike after strike, several can be in at once, and each goes again when its time is up (STORM TAKES). Turn one of its knobs and it stays. It lends RING, ROSE, COMP or VOWL, which need no delay memory. If the DSP has no room, that strike lends nothing.
  • HELI (helium, on the MIX page): a high-pass filter (it removes lows) up to about 200 Hz.
  • FOG, WIND, HAIL, AURORA and the TWISTER: each puts an effect of its own into an empty slot of the chain ({{pg:wxfx}}).

WSND on the MIX page (knob ((F))) turns all of this off: nothing of it is heard. The weather still moves the knobs.

The weather never touches the master: the freeze delay, the master EQ and the limiter stay where you set them (chapter 12).

  1. The weather comes on. The sky takes the last empty slot of the chain and puts the weather's effect into it. The popup says its name and IN, for example ``AERIA WIND IN``.
  2. It fades in. The effect's first knob, AMT, is turned up from 0% to 100% in about a third of a second.
  3. The weather goes off. AMT is turned down again. The effect is left to finish what it is doing: half a second for WIND, FOG and the TWISTER, two seconds for the hailstones still in the air, twelve seconds for the aurora's tail.
  4. The slot is emptied. The popup says ``NONE IN``.
  • Where it sits matters. The effect works at its slot's place in the chain: the effects before it feed it, the effects after it work on what it makes. To choose the place, leave only that slot empty.
  • No empty slot: the sky still shows the weather, and its sound is left out. Empty a slot and the effect comes at once.
  • No room in the DSP: the weather's sound is left out too. The slot's ring blinks for about five seconds and the popup says ``NO ROOM`` ``DSP FULL``, once: the sky does not ask again until that weather has been switched off and on again, so make room first ({{pg:dsproom}}). With VERB and ECHO both in the chain there is no room for HAIL, AURORA or the TWISTER. Beside SMEAR, HAIL and AURORA often find none: in the measured chain of five, WIND and the TWISTER fitted, HAIL and AURORA did not.
  • Yours stays yours. If the effect is already in the chain because you took it from the shelf, the weather leaves it alone: it neither turns its AMT nor takes it away. And if you put something else into the slot the weather is using, the weather lets go of it.
  • The other knobs of a weather's effect stay at their defaults while the weather owns it: the first six weathers do not move them, so the sky's twister lasts its 10 to 20 seconds. You may turn them yourself.
  • The slot lightning lends into is left to the lightning.

  • Trigs 1 to 7, T1 to T7, or ◀ ▶ select a slot.

Effects start dry

Dry means none of the effect is heard: its MIX is at 0. When AERIA first starts, every effect in the chain is held dry, and PITCH's SHFT is held at no shift. The same is done to any effect whose knobs nobody had turned in a saved state or a preset from an older version (chapter 17). Nothing is mangled before you ask for it.

Select the effect and turn MIX (knob F) to bring it in.

The held MIX stays at 0 until you turn it. The weather never moves a MIX, whatever is turned on or off (chapter 5). SHFT is not a weather knob and stays where it is.

An effect you take from the shelf, or put back after a bypass, is not held dry. It comes in at its default MIX, the one in the tables of chapter 7, and stays there until you turn it. Its MIX bowl may still carry the weather's mark; the weather does not move it.

Seven of the effects have more settings than six knobs can hold: a mode, a freeze, a make-up gain, a stereo width. Hold FUNC on CHAIN and the bowls change to the selected effect's second set. Turn the knob under a bowl to change it; the popup shows the value by name or in real units. An effect with nothing more shows NO MORE KNOBS. These settings are saved with the state and with projects. The weather does not move them, and a scene cannot hold them. Chapter 7 lists them for each effect.

The shelf

The shelf is the list of effects. Open it for the selected slot with BANK, trig 8 or trig 12, or with YES on an empty slot. Turn knob A or LEVEL, or press ▲ ▼, to choose. YES puts the effect in the slot. NO goes back. The first line, a dash, empties the slot.

The popup says the effect's name and IN when it is in place. Eleven effects use memory of their own: PITCH, GRAINS, RESO, SMEAR, VERB and ECHO, and the weather's WIND, FOG, TWISTER, HAIL and AURORA. Each of these can be in the chain only once; a second copy is refused. The others (DIST, RING, ROSE, COMP, VOWL) can be used several times.

The last five on the shelf are the weather's effects. The sky puts them into empty slots by itself while their weather lasts ({{pg:wxfx}}), and you can take them from the shelf like any other. Taken by hand, such an effect is yours: its AMT stays at 0%, where it does nothing, until you turn it.

HAIL and AURORA share one memory, and so do WIND and FOG. The weather never has both of a pair on, and the shelf does not take both by hand: with FOG in the chain a pick of WIND is refused, and FOG with WIND there; the same for HAIL and AURORA. The refusal reads ``NO ROOM`` ``DSP FULL``.

Where effects run, and how many fit

The unit's sound processor, the DSP, has two cores. Every effect costs a core some of its time, and a core can only do so much for each sample. AERIA puts each effect on a core that has room. You do not choose the core.

  • GRAINS, RESO, WIND and FOG always run on the first core. VERB, ECHO, TWISTER, HAIL and AURORA always run on the second. Their memories are there.
  • The other seven may run on either core. AERIA puts each on the core with more room, and moves one across later if that makes room for an effect that has no choice. SMEAR is one of the seven. It usually runs on the second core; the first core can take it only when no other effect is there.
  • When an effect fits on neither core, the pick is refused. The slot's ring blinks for about five seconds, then the popup says NO ROOM, DSP FULL. The slot stays as it was. A refused second copy of a delay-memory effect gives the same message.

How many sounds at once

Everything played from sample memory shares eight voices: the loop, each star that is sounding, each balloon grain, each PONG sample, and a file you are hearing in the card browser. While a computer is recording AERIA's audio over USB there are six.

  • A balloon uses up to four voices, one for each grain. When all the voices are in use, each balloon keeps one grain going and waits with the rest.
  • When all are in use and a new star or PONG sample is due, it takes the place of the quietest sound of its own kind. If there is none, it takes the quietest sound that is not the loop.

Bass mono

Below 50 Hz the left and the right side of the main output are made the same: the lows are mono. Wide effects and hard-panned tracks keep their width above that, while the bass stays in the middle, where a club system, a vinyl cut or a single subwoofer wants it. It sits after the EQ and before the limiter.

  • There is no knob and no switch for it.
  • It stands back while anything is routed to the cue pair: an effect slot at CUE or QUAD in the sky ({{ch:out}}), or a sequencer track with OUT at CUE or BOTH ({{ch:seqpages}}). Then the main pair is left as it is, so that four outputs used as four speakers stay four separate signals.
  • It acts on the main pair only. The cue pair never passes through it.

The limiter

A limiter stops the output from going over a ceiling, so loud moments do not clip. AERIA's sits last on the main output. LIMT on the FUNC layer of MIX switches it; it is on at first.

  • The ceiling is -1 dBFS: one dB under the loudest level the output can carry. Nothing passes it.
  • It looks ahead by 1.5 ms, so it has already turned down when a peak arrives. It recovers in 150 ms.
  • Because of the look-ahead, the main output is 1.5 ms later than in earlier versions. This is so whether LIMT is on or off.

  • Trigs 1 to 16 set TIME. The lamps show it as a bar.

What to know

  • In the sky the delay takes the chain's output, mixed to mono. In the sequencer it takes what each track sends with its DLY knob ({{ch:seqpages}}). The first echo comes back on the left, the next on the right.
  • The longest delay is 16 sixteenths at the tempo: 4 seconds at 60 BPM, 2 seconds at 120. It has 6 seconds of memory for each side.
  • Change TIME or the tempo while it plays and the delay fades from the old time to the new one. The pitch does not bend.
  • Change them while it is frozen and the loop jumps to the new length at once.
  • The tempo is shared with the sequencer. The delay counts its time at TMPO; it does not follow a MIDI clock coming in, even while the sequencer does ({{ch:seqsong}}). MARBLES keeps its own RATE.

The order at the output

The chain's output, with the weather's filters (in the sequencer: the eight tracks) → plus the freeze delay's echoes → OUT and BAL → the master EQ → bass mono → the limiter → the MAIN outputs. USB audio records the main output at the very end of this line ({{ch:usb}}). The drawings are in {{ch:overview}}.

The freeze delay, the EQ, bass mono and the limiter belong to the MAIN outputs. The CUE outputs do not pass through them.

Scenes in the sequencer

In the sequencer, hold [SCENE A] or [SCENE B] and turn a track knob on SRC, AMP, MOD, FLT, GEN or MIDI: the value goes into the scene at that end, for that track (the popup says SCENE A or SCENE B and the value). A scene is a set of knob positions. The crossfader holds one scene at its left end, A, and another at its right end, B, and moves the sound from one to the other. It works as it does on the original Octatrack.

There are sixteen scenes. At first A holds scene 1 and B holds scene 2, and every scene is empty. An empty scene changes nothing.

Putting a knob into a scene

  1. Hold SCENE A. The popup says SCENE A IS and the number of the scene that A holds.
  2. Still holding it, turn a knob. The popup shows the knob's name, the letter A and the value. That value now belongs to the scene. The knob's own setting has not moved.
  3. Let go. Do the same with SCENE B for the other end.

What the crossfader does

  • At the A end, every knob that scene A holds stands at the scene's value.
  • At the B end, every knob that scene B holds stands at scene B's value.
  • In between, each knob glides in a straight line from one to the other.
  • A knob that only one of the two scenes holds glides between that scene's value and the knob's own setting.
  • A knob that neither scene holds is left alone.

What a track is

Each of the eight tracks is its own channel into the sound processor. A track has:

  • one voice, made by its machine: a sample from the card (SMPL), a sine with a falling pitch (SUB), two sines in frequency modulation (FM), noise (NOIS), a plucked string (PLUK), or no sound at all, for a track that only plays another instrument over MIDI (MIDI);
  • a high-pass and a low-pass filter that are always there, with the resonance fixed at 20%;
  • room for one effect from the list of {{ch:fx}};
  • its own volume, pan, length, speed and direction, a send to the master's freeze delay, and a choice of output pair;
  • a MIDI note or chord it can send with every step.

A step is a sixteenth note. A track is 1 to 64 steps long, so up to four bars, and every track has its own length. There are four patterns, A to D. The timing is exact to the sample: swing, micro-timing and ratchets do not wobble with the screen. The signal's way is drawn on {{pg:flowseq}}.

While the sequencer is showing, the chain's effects and the three sources of the sky are not heard. Inputs A to D still pass to the output, dry. The master section is shared: the freeze delay, the master EQ, bass mono and the limiter work on the sequencer too ({{ch:mix}}).

GRID: setting steps

GRID is how the sequencer starts. The sixteen trigs are the steps of the page you see, for the track in hand.

  • Tap a trig to set a step. It plays at velocity 100. Tap a set step to clear it, with everything it held.
  • Hold a trig and the six knobs become that step's own: the STEP knobs, below. Hold several and they all change together. A step you turn a knob on is not cleared when you let go, and an empty step you turn a knob on becomes a trig.
  • The lamps show the steps that are set, the beats and the playhead ({{pg:seqlamps}}).

Ratchets and their curve

A ratchet plays the step several times inside the step's own length. Up to 8 it is a roll. The larger numbers (12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64) come so fast that they turn into a buzz or a pitch: at 120 BPM a step lasts an eighth of a second, so 64 hits in it sound as a tone of about 510 Hz.

  • CURV bends the row of hits. Toward IN the hits come faster and faster; toward OUT they start close together and spread. The number, 1 to 7, is how strongly: at 1 each gap is about seven eighths of the one before, at 7 a little over a third. For ratchets of more than 8 hits the bend is made gentler, so that the row still fills the step.
  • The row always fits the step exactly, whatever the curve.
  • Every hit of a ratchet carries the step's velocity, its direction and its locks, and sends the track's MIDI note if it has one.
  • With SLIP up on the SRC page, the start of the sample walks on with every hit of the roll.
  • Hits cannot come closer than about a thousandth of a second.

Latency

The sequencer makes its sound a little ahead of time, so that the stream to the sound processor never runs dry: a cushion of about 17 thousandths of a second (it grows to about 29 while the card browser or PROJECTS is open). A pad, like a step, is therefore heard about 17 ms after you hit it. What you record live is put back by that much, so it lands in the pattern where you heard it. MIDI notes and the MIDI clock are held back by the same time, so they leave the unit at the moment their sound does.

Patterns and chains

There are four patterns, A to D. They share the tracks' sounds and effects; each has its own steps, its own track lengths and its own PLEN.

  • Hold [PTN] and press [TRIG 1] to [TRIG 4]. The grid shows that pattern at once, so you can work on it. If the sequencer is playing, the change is heard when the playing pattern reaches the end of its PLEN: the top line says A>B until then.
  • When the pattern changes, every track starts from its first step, except tracks with FREE on.
  • A chain: hold [PTN] and press several trigs in a row, up to eight, for example 1 1 2 1. Let go and the popup says ``CHAIN SET``. The top line shows CH. The patterns play in that order, each for its PLEN, round and round. [PLAY] starts the chain from its first pattern.
  • Choosing a single pattern ends the chain.
  • To copy a pattern into another, see {{pg:copy}}.

While the sequencer is stopped, the letter in the top line is the pattern that last played. The grid always shows the pattern you chose.

Length, pages and polymetre

  • Every track has its own LEN in every pattern. Give one track 12 steps and another 16, and they drift against each other and meet again after 48.
  • A track longer than 16 has more than one page. [LEFT] [RIGHT] turn the page; the top line says which, as 2/2.
  • PLEN does not cut the tracks short. It only says when the pattern is over, for changing to another.
  • RATE on the GEN page gives a track its own clock as well ({{pg:gen}}): twelve steps at 3/2 against sixteen at X1.

Swing and shuffle

Swing plays every second step (2, 4, 6 …) late. 50% is straight. 66% is a triplet shuffle. 80% is the most. SWNG on PTN is for the whole pattern. SHUF on GEN gives one track a swing of its own, so the hats can lean while the kick stays straight. For one step alone, use MICR.

Stems over USB

The sequencer can send each track to a computer on its own stereo pair, beside the main mix: eight stems, for mixing a recorded jam afterwards. It is a second format of the USB audio interface, with 18 channels. How to choose it, and the channel list, are on {{pg:usbstems}}. In short:

  • A stem is tapped after the track's effect and before its pan. The filters and the effect are in it; PAN, OUT and the master section are not.
  • A track without an effect is mono: its stem is the same on both channels.
  • The freeze delay's echoes are in the main pair only.
  • While stems stream, the screen is redrawn more slowly. The sound is not affected.

Set a track's OUT (FLT page, knob D) to QUAD. The MAIN pair is then the front and the CUE pair the back. PAN places the track left to right and Y (knob F) front to back, so with speakers on all four outputs a track can sit anywhere in the room, and a lock on PAN or Y moves single steps. With only the MAIN pair connected, Y simply fades the track out toward the back.

While any track is at CUE, BOTH or QUAD the main output's bass mono stands back ({{pg:bassmono}}): the pair is then a front, not a left and right.

Playing the tracks from MIDI

Connect a keyboard or another sequencer to MIDI In. A note on channel 1 to 8 plays track 1 to 8. Note 60 (middle C) is the track's own pitch; every semitone above or below moves the pitch one semitone, as far as PTCH goes. The note's velocity is the hit's level. In LIVE recording ([REC]) with the sequencer playing, each note is written into the pattern at its place, with its pitch as a lock on that step.

  • A track whose machine is MIDI is never played from MIDI In, so a cable from MIDI Out back to MIDI In cannot make a track play itself.
  • NIN on the MIDI page's second set ([MIDI] twice, knob C) switches this off for the whole sequence.

What is kept

The sequence is in the unit's battery-backed memory. Every change is kept as you make it, and is there after the power has been off. If you switch off in the sequencer, AERIA OS starts in the sequencer, stopped. A project holds a copy of all of it ({{ch:projects}}). What is copied with [FUNC] + [REC] is not kept through a power-off.

What a stem is

  • Each stem is its track after its filters and its effect, before its pan. PAN, the OUT choice, the track's place in the main mix and the master section are not in it. A track's volume, its drive, its bits and the ducking are.
  • A track with no effect is mono, the same on both channels of its pair. A track with an effect carries the effect's stereo picture.
  • The delay's echoes are heard in MAIN only; there is no stem for the delay.
  • A muted track sends nothing into its stem.
  • Outside the sequencer (in the sky), the 18-channel format carries the main pair on 1 and 2 and silence on the rest.
  • While stems are streaming, the screen is redrawn more slowly: the main processor is busy moving sixteen more channels. The sound and the timing do not change.
  • In the heaviest case tried (eight tracks, MIDI chords on all of them, four effects, the delay at full feedback), 0.9% of the stems' frames were skipped while the live sound stayed whole ({{ch:specs}}). For a recording that matters, keep the load a little lower or record the main pair as well.

LIFE is Conway's Game of Life. The screen is a world of 64 by 32 cells, each two pixels square, and its edges meet: what leaves one side comes back on the other. Ten times a second every cell looks at its eight neighbours. An empty cell with exactly three live neighbours comes alive. A live cell with two or three stays alive. Every other cell dies or stays empty. When the world has stopped changing for a few seconds, has died out, or has run for 1,500 steps, it is seeded again: about a third of the cells, at random.

CLOCK is the XXIIVV clock, after Devine Lu Linvega's clock (clock.xxiivv.com). It shows decimal time, called Neralie time: the day is cut into 1,000 beats of 86.4 seconds, and each beat into 1,000 pulses of 86.4 thousandths of a second. The time is written under the face as beats:pulses, six figures. 000:000 is midnight and 500:000 is noon.

The face is a rectangle cut by six nested lines, one for each figure. The first runs across at the height of the day so far, top to bottom. The second drops from it, the third crosses from that, and so on inward. Each line moves ten times as fast as the one before. A dashed line carries the second line up to the top edge.

Updating and recovery

[[PANEL FUNC TRIG2=never | The way back [FUNC] held while switching on :: The Elektron Startup Menu. It is always there: AERIA OS never writes to the bootloader [TRIG 2] in that menu :: EMPTY RESET. Do not press it unless you mean to wipe the unit ]]
These tools talk to the unit over USB while it runs. Do not use them while you are playing.

Updating AERIA OS over USB

With AERIA running and connected, the link tool writes a new version into the unit's flash memory and checks every byte:

aos_flash.py AERIA_OS.bin MAIN.raw — then switch the Octatrack off and on.

The updater never touches the Elektron bootloader (the first 16 KB of flash). It writes the new OS back to front, header last, so an interrupted update can never look like a valid OS. Keep the power on while it writes (about ten seconds).

Testing without writing

aos.py boot MAIN.raw runs a build from RAM only. Switching off returns to the version in flash.

If AERIA OS will not start

Hold FUNC while switching on: the Elektron Startup Menu appears. Choose MIDI UPGRADE and send AERIA_OS.syx (or any Octatrack OS .syx file) into the MIDI IN socket from a MIDI interface on your computer. The unit's USB socket is not used for this. Do not press TRIG 2 (EMPTY RESET).

Going back to the Octatrack's own operating system

The same way: hold FUNC while switching on, choose MIDI UPGRADE, and send Elektron's own Octatrack MKII OS .syx file into MIDI IN. AERIA OS is replaced; the bootloader, as always, is untouched. Projects and the saved state of AERIA OS live in the unit's battery-backed memory. Whether they are still there after a stay in the Octatrack's own system has not been tested: do not count on it.
